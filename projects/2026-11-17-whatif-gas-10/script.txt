What if gas hit ten dollars a gallon?

Today, the national average is about three dollars and seventy-eight cents. So let's turn up the dial.

Week one. Six dollars. Meet Dana. Her fifteen-gallon fill-up used to cost about fifty-seven dollars. Now? Ninety.

Real fact: the all-time record was about five dollars and one cent, back in June 2022. We just blew right past it.

Month two. Eight dollars. Trucks moved more than eleven billion tons of freight last year, and every one of them needs fuel. So the prices on the shelf start creeping up too.

Month three. Ten dollars. The average driver covers about thirteen thousand seven hundred miles a year. At twenty-five miles per gallon, that's about twenty-one hundred dollars of gas today. At ten dollars? About fifty-five hundred. An extra thirty-four hundred dollars, every year.

So who wins? Electric cars. People who work from home. And oil producers.

Who loses? Long commutes. Delivery drivers. And anyone who lives far from work.
