In 1976, a man sold his ten percent of Apple for 800 dollars. Today, ten percent of Apple is worth about 500 billion dollars.

That's not a typo. And he's not the only one. History is full of tiny deals that turned into giant regrets. So today, we're pulling five old receipts out of the drawer and running each one through a time machine, to see what it would be worth now.

Here's how the machine works. We take the price printed on each receipt, and compare it with what the same thing is worth today. No tricks, just two numbers and a lot of zeros.

Stay to the end, because the last receipt does something strange. It shrinks.

Receipt number one. May 22nd, 2010. Two pizzas.

A programmer wanted to prove that bitcoin could buy real things. So he offered 10,000 bitcoin to anyone who would bring him two pizzas. Someone did. At the time, those 10,000 bitcoin were worth about 41 dollars.

Now let's run that receipt through the machine. On November 9th, one bitcoin was trading at around 74,200 dollars. So those two pizzas cost about 742 million dollars. That's roughly 18 million times what they were worth back then. Probably the most expensive dinner ever eaten. Today, crypto fans still celebrate May 22nd as Bitcoin Pizza Day.

Receipt number two. The year 2000. A video rental giant meets a tiny DVD-by-mail startup.

According to Netflix's founders, they offered to sell Netflix to Blockbuster for 50 million dollars. Blockbuster passed. Its former boss later said the talks were never that serious. Either way, the deal didn't happen.

Time machine. Netflix is now worth about 260 billion dollars. That's more than 5,000 times the asking price. And Blockbuster? It filed for bankruptcy in 2010. Today, there is exactly one Blockbuster store left, in Bend, Oregon.

Receipt number three. 1999. You could have bought Google for 750 thousand dollars.

Google's founders offered their little search engine to Excite, one of the biggest websites of the day. The price started at one million dollars. Then it dropped to 750 thousand. Excite's boss said no.

Time machine. Google's parent company, Alphabet, is now worth about 3.5 trillion dollars. That's about 4.7 million times more than the price Excite turned down.

Receipt number four. Back to where we started. April 1976.

Apple had three founders. Two of them, you know. The third, Ronald Wayne, owned ten percent. Just twelve days later, he sold it back for 800 dollars. He was worried about being personally on the hook for the young company's debts.

Time machine. Apple is worth about 4.98 trillion dollars. Ten percent of that is about 498 billion dollars. Now, to be fair, his slice would have shrunk over the years, as Apple raised money and issued more stock. Fortune estimates it would really be worth somewhere between 75 and 360 billion dollars. Still, not bad for 800 dollars.

Receipt number five. And this is the one that shrinks.

February 2008. Microsoft offers to buy Yahoo for 44.6 billion dollars. Yahoo says no. It believes it's worth more.

Eight years later, Yahoo agreed to sell its main business to Verizon for about 4.8 billion dollars. Roughly a tenth of the offer it turned down. The time machine doesn't always make things bigger.

Let's line up the scoreboard. Two pizzas: about 18 million times. Blockbuster's chance at Netflix: more than 5,000 times. Excite's chance at Google: about 4.7 million times. Ronald Wayne's slice of Apple: hundreds of billions, even after dilution. And Yahoo: down to about a tenth.

So here's the twist. Looking back, every one of these people looks foolish. But at the time, almost every choice made sense.

In 2010, one bitcoin was worth less than a penny. In 2000, Netflix was a small company losing money. In 1999, Google was just one search engine among many. And in 1976, Apple was a tiny startup in a garage. Nobody gets to read tomorrow's receipt.

Hindsight is the real time machine. And Yahoo is the reminder that saying no can be expensive too.
