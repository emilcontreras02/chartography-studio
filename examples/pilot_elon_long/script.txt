Elon Musk is the richest person in the world. As of November first, Forbes puts his fortune at around 869 billion dollars.

So here's a question you've probably asked yourself at least once. What if he just... gave it all away? Every last dollar, split evenly. Would it end poverty? Would we all be rich? Or would something strange happen?

Let's do the math. Because the answer is not what most people expect.

First, let's get a feel for how big 869 billion dollars actually is.

The second richest person on Earth, Jeff Bezos, is worth about 361 billion. Musk has more than twice that. In fact, his fortune is bigger than the next three richest people combined.

Here's another way to see it. The entire United States government, the military, Social Security, everything, spent about 7.4 trillion dollars in its last fiscal year. That's roughly 20 billion dollars a day. Musk's whole fortune would keep the US government running for about 43 days.

Hold on to that number. 43 days. It matters later.

Now, the big split. There are about 8.3 billion people alive right now.

Take 869 billion dollars, divide it by 8.3 billion people, and every single person on Earth gets about 104 dollars.

That's it. One hundred and four dollars.

For a lot of people watching this, that's a nice dinner out, maybe a new pair of shoes. Pleasant, but it doesn't change your life.

But for the hundreds of millions of people living on less than 3 dollars a day, 104 dollars is more than a month of living. Same money, completely different meaning, depending on where you were born.

Okay. What if he only gave it to Americans?

The US has about 342 million people. Split 869 billion dollars between them, and every American, every adult and every kid, gets about 2,540 dollars.

And here's where it gets interesting. America has actually tried something like this before. During the pandemic, the government sent out three rounds of stimulus checks. All together, about 476 million payments, worth roughly 814 billion dollars.

That's almost exactly Musk's fortune.

So we don't have to guess what happens. We've seen it. People paid bills, paid down debt, and spent it. Some economists argue those checks, together with everything else happening at the time, helped push prices up in 2021 and 2022. Others say broken supply chains were the bigger cause. Either way, one burst of cash didn't make anyone rich for long.

So what if we aimed it where it matters most?

The World Bank says people living on less than 3 dollars a day are in extreme poverty. By its latest count, that's around 838 million people.

Give Musk's fortune only to them, and each person gets about 1,040 dollars.

At 3 dollars a day, that's enough to lift every one of them above the extreme poverty line for about 346 days. Almost a full year.

That's genuinely powerful. But notice the word. A year. Not forever. On day 347, the money runs out, and the things that kept people poor, no jobs, no roads, no schools, no stable government, are still there.

But here's the twist most people miss. Musk doesn't actually have 869 billion dollars.

Not in cash, anyway.

His wealth is mostly ownership. According to Forbes, nearly four fifths of it is his stake in SpaceX. Most of the rest is Tesla. To hand out cash, he'd have to sell those shares. And to sell them, someone has to buy them.

We just watched what happens when SpaceX stock gets sold off. After the company went public in June, its shares climbed fast, and Musk's net worth passed 1.3 trillion dollars. Then the selling started. In about six weeks, SpaceX shares fell 46 percent, and more than 600 billion dollars of Musk's fortune disappeared.

Think about that. Split 600 billion dollars across everyone on Earth, and it's about 72 dollars each. He lost most of your hundred dollar share, without giving anyone a single cent.

Now imagine him trying to sell everything at once. The flood of shares would crash the price long before he finished. By the time the money reached you, your 104 dollars might be 40. Or 20.

His fortune is real in the way a house price is real. It's what someone would pay for it today. Try to sell every house on the street on the same afternoon, and that price is gone.

So, what would actually happen if Elon Musk gave everyone his money?

Everyone on Earth gets about 104 dollars, and probably much less after the crash. Americans get a stimulus check they've basically seen before. The world's poorest get a year of breathing room. And then, in most places, life goes back to normal.

Remember those 43 days? That's the real lesson. The biggest fortune in history is enormous for one person, but small next to the world's economy, or even one government's yearly budget. One person's money, given once, can't fix what whole systems create every single year.

That doesn't mean it would be worthless. Aimed carefully, at clean water, vaccines, and schools, 869 billion dollars could change millions of lives for good. It just wouldn't make all of us rich.
